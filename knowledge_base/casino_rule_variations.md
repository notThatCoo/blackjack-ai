# Casino Rule Variations and Their Effect on House Edge

## Overview

Not every blackjack table plays the same. Rule variations shift the house edge and sometimes change the correct basic strategy play. Knowing what to look for lets you find the best tables before sitting down.

A baseline 6-deck H17 game with standard rules has a house edge of roughly 0.64% with perfect basic strategy.

---

## Key Rule Variations

### Dealer Hits or Stands on Soft 17

| Rule | Effect on House Edge |
|------|---------------------|
| Dealer Stands on Soft 17 (S17) | Better for player (~-0.20%) |
| Dealer Hits Soft 17 (H17) | Worse for player |

H17 gives the dealer an extra chance to improve a weak hand. Always prefer S17 tables when available.

---

### Number of Decks

| Decks | House Edge (approx, all else equal) |
|-------|-------------------------------------|
| 1     | 0.17% |
| 2     | 0.46% |
| 4     | 0.60% |
| 6     | 0.64% |
| 8     | 0.66% |

Fewer decks = lower house edge. Prefer single or double deck when available, but watch for offsetting rules casinos often add (like 6:5 blackjack payouts on single-deck games).

---

### Blackjack Payout

| Payout | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| 3:2    | Standard — expected |
| 6:5    | Adds ~1.39% to house edge — avoid |
| Even money | Never take |

**6:5 blackjack is one of the worst rules you will encounter.** It is common on single-deck games at large casinos. A 3:2 six-deck game is better than a 6:5 single-deck game.

---

### Double Down Rules

| Rule | Effect on House Edge |
|------|---------------------|
| Double on any two cards | Standard |
| Double on 9-11 only | +0.09% against player |
| Double on 10-11 only | +0.18% against player |

More doubling freedom is better for the player. The ability to double soft hands is particularly valuable.

---

### Double After Split (DAS)

| Rule | Effect |
|------|--------|
| DAS allowed | -0.14% (better for player) |
| DAS not allowed | Worse for player |

DAS lets you double down on each hand after splitting a pair. Prefer tables that offer it.

**Strategy impact**: With DAS, split 2,2 and 3,3 against dealer 2 and 3 (instead of hitting).

---

### Late Surrender

| Rule | Effect |
|------|--------|
| Late surrender offered | -0.08% (better for player) |
| No surrender | Standard in most games |

Late surrender lets you fold after the dealer checks for blackjack, recovering half your bet. Most valuable on hard 15 and 16 vs strong dealer upcards.

**Key surrender plays (6-deck H17)**:
- Hard 16 vs 9, 10, Ace → Surrender
- Hard 15 vs 10 → Surrender

---

### Early Surrender

Early surrender (fold before dealer checks for blackjack) is extremely rare and very valuable — roughly -0.6% to house edge. You will almost never see this in a standard casino.

---

## Reading a Table Before You Sit Down

When you walk up to a blackjack table at a casino, check these things before putting any money down:

1. **Blackjack pays?** — Look for "Blackjack pays 3 to 2" on the felt. Walk away from 6:5.
2. **How many decks?** — Look at the shoe size or ask the dealer.
3. **Does dealer hit soft 17?** — Usually printed on the felt ("Dealer must hit soft 17" or "Dealer stands on all 17s").
4. **Is surrender allowed?** — Ask the dealer.
5. **Is DAS allowed?** — Usually yes, but worth confirming.

This is the first thing a smart player does before sitting down. A bad table with poor rules can wipe out the advantage you gain from counting.

---

## Best Rule Combinations to Look For

Ideal (rarely found all together):
- 3:2 blackjack payout
- S17 (dealer stands on soft 17)
- DAS (double after split allowed)
- Late surrender offered
- 1 or 2 decks

Realistic target at most casinos:
- 3:2 payout
- 6 decks, H17
- DAS allowed
- Late surrender available

Even this combination, with perfect basic strategy and card counting, gives the player a long-run edge.
